# Camera moves

Fill one move. The subject clause comes from video-prompt when that skill is already writing the clip. Examples are quality targets, not text to reuse.

## Push in

{Subject} {small movement}. The camera slowly pushes in toward {subject} to emphasize {detail}.

Use it for a face, a logo, a texture, or the one action that matters. A slow push-in is the default when they asked for a camera and named no move.

Weak, because the clip stacks three moves and a quality tag:

```text
The camera dollies in, pans right, and tilts up toward the statue, cinematic, 8K.
```

The same idea as one move:

```text
The statue stands still. The camera slowly pushes in to emphasize the folds of the drapery.
```

A product detail:

```text
The bottle stays on the table. The camera slowly pushes in to emphasize the cap and the liquid line.
```

## Pull back

{Subject} {small movement}. The camera slowly pulls back from {close subject} to reveal {environment}.

Name what the frame starts on and what enters as it retreats. If there is nothing to reveal, use a push-in instead. An aerial pull-back that rises while it reveals is one move. Write it as a pull-back.

```text
The ring stays in frame. The camera slowly pulls back to reveal the white table and the rest of the display.
```

```text
The man stands still on the roof. The camera pulls back to reveal the skyline behind him.
```

## Pan

{Subject} {movement along the pan, or holds still}. The camera slowly pans {left or right} across {destination}, stable.

Use it for a lineup, a desk, a street, or to travel from a person to what they are looking at. The subject should not race the opposite way.

```text
The objects on the desk stay put. The camera slowly pans left to right across the keyboard, the monitor, and the headphones, stable.
```

```text
The cyclist rides along the street. The camera pans right to follow him, stable.
```

## Tilt

{Subject} {small movement}. The camera tilts {up or down} from {lower or higher detail} to {the other end}, stable.

The picture needs a top and a bottom: shoes to face, a pendant to a table, the street to the roofline. If it does not, pick a pan or a push-in.

```text
The athlete stands still. The camera tilts up from the running shoes to the face, stable.
```

```text
The camera tilts down from the pendant light to the table setting, stable.
```

## Track

{Subject} {travels}. The camera tracks {forward or alongside} at the same pace, stable.

Say which way both are going. A track alongside stays at one distance. A track forward follows from behind.

```text
The cyclist pedals down the road. The camera tracks alongside, low and stable.
```

## Orbit

{Subject} {holds still or turns slowly}. The camera orbits slowly around {subject} to show {the side or the back}.

Do not combine an orbit with a push-in and a tilt in the same shot. A wide orbit around a product invents the hidden side and warps the label. Keep the arc short, and say the product stays rigid. Prefer a push-in when the label must stay exact.

```text
The bottle stays rigid and centered. The camera orbits a short way, and the label stays readable.
```

## Static

{Subject} {the movement that carries the shot}. Static camera.

Use this when they asked the camera to hold, or when a shot in a list is only there to show the action.

```text
The creator walks into the studio. Static camera.
```

## Reveal

Start on {close or wide view of the subject}. The camera {one move} to reveal {end view}.

This is a pull-back, a tilt, or a pan with the start and end stated. It is still one move. Two attached frames stay a video-prompt start-and-end job. Add this clause only for how the camera connects them.

```text
Start on the watch face. The camera pulls back to reveal the wrist and the dark background.
```

## Shot list

One line per shot: shot size, subject movement, one camera move. Repeat the subject so identity holds. Give each shot a duration under **Settings** or at the start of the line when they named a multi-shot mode.

```text
Shot 1, 3 seconds: Wide shot. The creator walks into the studio. Static camera.
Shot 2, 4 seconds: She sits at the desk. The camera slowly pushes in.
Shot 3, 3 seconds: The camera pans right across the monitor, stable.
```
